No streaks. No badges. No red.

Skip a day and nothing happens. Skip a week and nothing happens. Missed prompts stack quietly and expire after two weeks, and the app never mentions it again.

There is no counter to break, no confetti when you comply, and nothing in the interface turns red because you were busy.

An app that punishes a parent for a hard week is a bad parenting app. That's a design decision, not a missing feature.

03 · The archive

Built to be searched, not scrolled

A timeline with their age on every day

Scan it by date. Age is stamped on every entry, so you're not doing arithmetic to work out whether he was three or four. The point of an archive isn't that it's stored. It's that somebody can find something in it.

Search everything — even what was spoken

Everything you wrote, everything they said, and every prompt you were asked. Voice notes are transcribed on your phone, so a sentence forty seconds into a recording you'll never relisten to is still one search away.

The question and the answer, one object

Every entry keeps the prompt that caused it. And backdating is normal here, not a workaround — the day something happened and the day you got around to writing it down are two different facts, and both are kept.

04 · The promise

Your kid isn't the product

Most apps handle this badly, so here it is in full, before you sign up.

No ads. No data sale. Ever.

Not now, not at a bigger scale, not ever. Nothing about your child is sold, brokered, or used to train anything. Sign in with Apple, or Google. No Facebook login — routing a private record of a child through an ad company contradicts the thing being sold.

Private by default

Nobody sees the archive unless you invite them. There is no feed, no likes, and no place other people perform.

We have never deleted a word a parent wrote

If you stop paying, Dogear drops to the free tier. Nothing is locked. Your whole archive stays readable. You keep answering the daily question — text and did-it entries never stop. New photo and video uploads pause. After ninety days, original photos move to cheaper cold storage: thumbnails stay instant, search still works, and pulling an original back takes about an hour. That's a property of the storage, not a penalty.

Text is never deleted. Not ever. If media is ever going to be removed, you get warned three times — ninety, thirty, and seven days out — each with a free one-click export. Sign in once during that window and it's cancelled outright. If we can't reach you, we keep it.

An export you can't read isn't a backup

One tap gives you a zip: every entry, every photo, every recording, in folders by date — plus an index.html you double-click. It opens in any browser, offline, with no account and no internet. Free, complete, always — even if you stopped paying years ago. An archive meant to last eighteen years shouldn't depend on this company lasting eighteen years.

05 · The handoff

It isn't yours forever. That's the point.

Everything in here is being kept for one person, and it isn't you. When your family decides they're ready, they get all of it. Some things you can seal until a birthday you choose — a letter meant for eighteen stays shut until eighteen.
