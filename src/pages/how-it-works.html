How it works

{{PRODUCT}} is a daily prompt journal for parents. One question and one small mission arrive each morning; you answer in about fifteen seconds, one-handed, and the answer is filed with the question that caused it. That last part is the whole product.

So here is how it actually goes — laid out the way {{PRODUCT}} lays out everything: on a timeline. It starts tomorrow morning and runs to the morning they turn eighteen.

Tomorrow · 7:00 a.m.

The morning card

One notification, at a time you pick. Open it and there are two things: a question and a mission. Not a feed, not a dashboard, not eleven things you're behind on.

There are four kinds you'll see most days — a question, a capture mission, an activity with nothing to upload, and occasionally a letter. All {{PROMPT_COUNT}} are published, so you can read every one before you sign up for anything.

That week · that month

Missions: daily, weekly, monthly

Most days it's small. Once a week something slightly bigger lands — film the bedtime routine start to finish, one take. Once a month a measurement mission asks for a number: height, hand, foot, the things you'll want on a chart later and will never reconstruct from memory.

Monthly

Trace their hand on paper. Photograph it next to last month's.

A month, more or less: something small most days, one bigger mission on a weekend, one measurement at the end. The empty days cost nothing.

Whenever you get to it

Backdating, which is normal here

The day something happened and the day you got around to writing it down are two different facts, and {{PRODUCT}} keeps both. File a memory under the day it happened, whenever you get to it. Nothing marks it as late, because it isn't.

This is also the answer to “my kid is seven, is it too late?” — no. Plenty of a real archive will be older than the app that holds it.

Filed under Saturday, June 14 written June 29 First time to the top of the big slide. Went straight back up before I'd finished clapping. Two dates, both kept. Nothing marks it late.

Year three

The timeline

Everything lands in one place, grouped by date, with their age stamped on every day header. You are never doing arithmetic to work out whether he was three or four.

Year nine

Search, and why the prompt matters

Search runs across everything you wrote, every transcript of something they said, and every prompt you were asked. That third one is what nothing else has.

Searching a camera roll means searching dates, places and whatever the machine thinks it sees. Searching {{PRODUCT}} means searching the questions — “afraid of”, “pretending to be”, “wouldn't let us correct” — which is much closer to how you'll actually remember it in twenty years.

Any given night

Letters, sealed until an age you pick

Some prompts ask you to write forward — a letter to who they'll be at eighteen, or what you'd want them to know if you weren't around to say it. You choose when it opens. A letter meant for eighteen stays shut until eighteen.

Every day in between

Second parent, one archive

Both parents answer into the same archive, and it's one subscription, not two. Whoever was there that day writes it down. Neither of you has to reconstruct the other's week.

Year eighteen

Export, which is the actual promise

One tap gives you a zip: every entry, every photo, every recording, in folders by date — plus an index.html you double-click. It opens in any browser, offline, with no account and no internet. No app required, including this one.

Free, complete, always. Even on the free tier. Even if you stopped paying years ago. It's built that way because an archive meant to last eighteen years shouldn't depend on this company lasting eighteen years. The rest of that promise is here.
